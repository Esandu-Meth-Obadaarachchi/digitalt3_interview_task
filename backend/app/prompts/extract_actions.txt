You extract action items from one chunk of a meeting transcript.

An action item is a commitment to do something after this meeting. Someone
agrees to do a piece of work, or is asked to and does not refuse.

These are NOT action items:
- describing work already finished ("I pushed the migration scripts last night")
- describing work in progress with no new commitment ("I've been looking into it")
- a hypothetical ("we could switch to SSE")
- a decision about how to do something, with no owner taking it on
- general discussion, opinions, or a digression
- a summary or recap of commitments already made. When someone closes a meeting
  by restating what was agreed ("so to recap, Priya is finishing the refactor,
  James is setting up the pipeline"), those are the same commitments as before,
  not new ones. Extract a commitment from the moment it was made, not from the
  moment it was repeated back.

{context}

TRANSCRIPT CHUNK
The lines below are the only text you may quote. The context block above is
background and must never appear in a quote.

{chunk}

RULES, in order of importance:

1. verbatim_quote must be copied character for character from the TRANSCRIPT
   CHUNK above. Do not tidy it, do not fix grammar, do not join two lines, do
   not include the "[00:00:00] Name:" prefix. Copy the words only. A quote
   that is not an exact substring of the chunk will be rejected.

   Quote the words that make this a commitment. Where a person accepts work
   with a bare "Yeah, I can do that", quote the request instead, because the
   acceptance alone does not say what was agreed.

2. owner is the person who will do the work. Use the exact name as it appears
   in the participant list.
   If the transcript does not say who will do it, output exactly UNSPECIFIED.
   Never infer an owner from who is speaking, from who spoke last, or from who
   seems most likely. "Someone needs to check the migration path" has owner
   UNSPECIFIED.
   If a person assigns work to somebody else, the owner is the person the work
   was assigned to, not the speaker.

3. due_date is the timing stated for THIS commitment, copied as words, not
   converted.
   "by Friday" -> due_date "Friday"
   "by end of next week" -> due_date "end of next week"
   The timing must belong to this commitment. A passage often contains two
   commitments and one date: "I'll send out a poll by end of day. Lisa to
   coordinate the demo scheduling" gives the poll a date and the coordinating
   none. Do not borrow a date from a neighbouring sentence.
   If no timing is stated for this commitment, output exactly UNSPECIFIED.
   Never calculate a calendar date. Never invent one.

4. what is a short description of the work, in your own words, under 20 words.

5. speaker is who said the quote. timestamp is that line's timestamp, exactly
   as it appears in the chunk.

6. confidence is your honest probability from 0.0 to 1.0 that this is a real
   commitment. Use a low value when you are unsure rather than omitting the
   item, and a low value when the owner or the date was hard to determine.
   Do not return 0.95 for everything. An item where the owner was named
   explicitly and the work is unambiguous deserves a higher value than one you
   inferred from an indirect phrasing.

If the chunk contains no action items, return an empty list. An empty list is a
correct answer and is much better than a guess.
